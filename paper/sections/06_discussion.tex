\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What does the linear-probe gap measure?}
The probe is a single $1\times1\times1$ convolution with no nonlinearity.
Any non-trivial mIoU it achieves must originate from features the encoder
already provides in linearly separable form. A $2$--$3\times$ improvement
over random init at every label fraction indicates that OMU-MAE places a
substantial portion of supervision-relevant variance into the linear span
of its bottleneck.

\paragraph{What does the cross-modal target buy?}
Comparing OMU-MAE against the Occupancy-MAE baseline (truly LiDAR-only, no
DINOv2 input or feature head; otherwise identical architecture, masking,
and probe) isolates the cross-modal DINOv2 supervision. OMU-MAE improves
by $+1.77$\,pp at $100\%$ labels and by $+1.0$ to $+1.8$\,pp consistently
across fractions. The frozen-VFM target adds a modest but reliable
semantic signal on top of pure geometric occupancy reconstruction.

\paragraph{What does masking buy?}
Comparing OMU-MAE against the no-mask variant (full cross-modal input but
$\rho=0$, functionally a CleverDistiller-equivalent) isolates the masking
inductive bias. Masking helps by $+3.27$\,pp at $100\%$ and \emph{more} in
the low-label regime ($+5.17$\,pp at $1\%$): forcing the encoder to inpaint
missing voxel context from surrounding visible voxels yields features that
are more useful precisely when downstream labels are scarce.

\paragraph{Masked reconstruction vs.\ contrastive distillation.}
The head-to-head against the SLidR re-implementation is the most direct
test of our central claim. Both consume the \emph{same} frozen DINOv2
target; they differ only in objective (masked feature reconstruction in a
dense voxel grid vs.\ InfoNCE contrastive matching at sparse superpoint
resolution). OMU-MAE wins by $+2.52$ to $+4.22$\,pp, with the gap growing
as labels increase. Notably, in our dense-voxel probe the SLidR baseline
($15.46\%$) is also \emph{below} the LiDAR-only Occupancy-MAE
($17.91\%$): a dense masked-occupancy objective outperforms sparse
contrastive VFM distillation under this protocol. We read this as evidence
that dense, per-voxel reconstruction targets suit a dense voxel encoder
better than sparse superpoint-level contrastive objectives, rather than as
a claim that contrastive distillation is weak in general (SLidR still beats
random init by $+9.96$\,pp).

\paragraph{Why a frozen DINOv2 target?}
A frozen target cannot co-adapt to the predictor, so the trivial
constant-output collapse mode that affects joint-target prediction
methods~\cite{ijepa,lejepa} cannot achieve low loss. This sidesteps the
regularization machinery (VICReg, LeJEPA, I-JEPA-style asymmetries) needed
by approaches whose target evolves during training.
