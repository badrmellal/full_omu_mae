\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Masked autoencoding for LiDAR.}
The MAE paradigm~\cite{mae}---high-fraction masking of input patches with
a reconstruction objective---was extended to LiDAR by
Occupancy-MAE~\cite{occmae} and Voxel/LiDAR-MAE~\cite{hess2023lidar}.
Occupancy-MAE introduced range-aware masking (higher mask probability at
closer ranges, where LiDAR is denser) and predicts binary voxel occupancy
at masked positions. MAELi~\cite{maeli} scales these ideas to large LiDAR
sequences. OMU-MAE follows Occupancy-MAE's range-aware schedule but
\emph{adds} a second prediction target at masked positions: a
$768$-dimensional feature vector matching the frozen DINOv2 patch feature
projected from the paired camera image. The single-modality occupancy
reconstruction of Occupancy-MAE and the cross-modal feature
reconstruction of OMU-MAE are complementary, and we use both losses
jointly.

\paragraph{Multi-modal masked autoencoders.}
UniM\textsuperscript{2}AE~\cite{unim2ae} is the closest published
multi-modal MAE for autonomous driving. It \emph{jointly} trains both the
camera and LiDAR encoders, masks tokens in both modalities, and
reconstructs both modalities back to their \emph{raw} representations
through a Multi-modal 3D Interaction Module that exchanges features
between LiDAR and camera volumes via back-projection. OMU-MAE differs in
three ways: (i)~the camera encoder is \emph{frozen} (DINOv2) and never
receives gradient; (ii)~the cross-modal target is the \emph{frozen DINOv2
feature volume}, not raw pixels; and (iii)~there is no interaction module
or back-projection, only direct concatenation of the projected DINOv2
features into the voxel input. The trade-off is intentional:
UniM\textsuperscript{2}AE's joint training and interaction module are more
expressive but more compute-heavy. NS-MAE~\cite{nsmae} is a different
multi-modal MAE that reconstructs raw RGB pixels and raw LiDAR points
through a NeRF-style volumetric rendering decoder; OMU-MAE predicts a
fixed-grid voxel feature volume directly and does not use volumetric
rendering.

\paragraph{Vision-foundation-model distillation to LiDAR.}
SLidR~\cite{slidr}, ScaLR~\cite{scalr} and SuperFlow++~\cite{superflow}
distill features from a frozen image network into LiDAR superpoint
features through InfoNCE-style contrastive matching at sparse superpoint
resolution (typically $\sim$50 superpoints per scan).
CleverDistiller~\cite{cleverdistiller}, the most recent and closest,
replaces InfoNCE with a direct feature-similarity loss through an MLP
projection head and adds an auxiliary occupancy prediction task. The
combination of a frozen VFM target $+$ occupancy prediction is shared with
OMU-MAE. \emph{The differentiator is masked reconstruction.}
CleverDistiller distills VFM features at all 3D positions without masking;
OMU-MAE zeros out $85\%$ of voxel features and recovers them, forcing the
encoder to inpaint missing semantic context from surrounding visible
voxels. To make this comparison concrete rather than rhetorical, we
re-implement both a contrastive (SLidR) and a no-mask distillation
(CleverDistiller-equivalent) variant inside our framework and compare
head-to-head under an identical protocol (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}).

\paragraph{Joint-embedding predictive architectures.}
JEPA-family methods~\cite{ijepa,vjepa,lejepa,adljepa} predict latent
representations of masked targets rather than reconstructing raw pixels or
features. Both target and predictor evolve during training, which
empirically requires careful regularization (VICReg~\cite{vicreg},
LeJEPA~\cite{lejepa}) to avoid representational collapse. We deliberately
avoid this regime by using a frozen target that cannot co-adapt to the
predictor.

\paragraph{Dense vision features.}
DINO~\cite{dino}, DINOv2~\cite{dinov2} and DINOv3~\cite{dinov3} produce
dense per-patch features with strong emergent semantics, increasingly used
as a stable representation source by downstream methods. We use DINOv2
ViT-B/14 in this work for the quality of its dense features and the public
availability of weights via \texttt{torch.hub}.
