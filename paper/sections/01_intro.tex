\section{Introduction}

\input{sections/fig_teaser}

Self-supervised learning (SSL) has sharply reduced the labeling burden
in 2D perception~\cite{mae,dino,dinov2}, but transferring this success to
autonomous driving remains challenging. Driving scenes are inherently
multi-modal, combining camera images dense in semantics with LiDAR point
clouds dense in geometry. A useful pretrained representation must capture
\emph{both}, ideally without large GPU farms and without paired human
annotation.

Recent work spans roughly four cells of a $2\times2$ design grid, indexed
by (i)~training objective and (ii)~target source. We summarize this
design space in Table~\ref{tab:design}. The bottom-right cell---masked
reconstruction with a frozen vision foundation model (VFM) as
target---is the empty cell that motivates OMU-MAE. Closely related
methods occupy adjacent cells but make different design choices:
UniM\textsuperscript{2}AE~\cite{unim2ae} jointly trains both modality
encoders and reconstructs raw pixels and LiDAR; NS-MAE~\cite{nsmae}
reconstructs raw observations through volumetric NeRF rendering;
SLidR~\cite{slidr}, ScaLR~\cite{scalr} and CleverDistiller~\cite{cleverdistiller}
distill VFM features at \emph{all} positions without masking. We do not
claim this empty cell is fundamentally novel as a recipe---each of its
ingredients is well known---but we report a controlled end-to-end
empirical study of it on KITTI / SemanticKITTI, with a fair in-framework
head-to-head against the adjacent cells.

\paragraph{Two design choices and their motivation.}
First, a \emph{frozen} image encoder. A frozen target cannot co-adapt to
the predictor, ruling out the trivial constant-output collapse mode that
affects joint-target prediction methods~\cite{ijepa,lejepa}. The encoder
still receives strong, dense semantic supervision because DINOv2 features
are themselves a strong representation. Second, \emph{masked
reconstruction} rather than direct distillation. Masking forces the
encoder to inpaint missing voxel context from surrounding visible voxels,
an inductive bias that distillation at every position does not impose.
Whether this inductive bias actually improves downstream transfer
compared to a non-masked baseline is an open empirical question; we
answer it directly by re-implementing both a contrastive (SLidR) and a
non-masked distillation (CleverDistiller-equivalent) baseline on
identical data and protocol.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item We propose OMU-MAE, a voxel-level masked autoencoder whose target
combines binary occupancy and frozen DINOv2~\cite{dinov2} features
projected from the paired camera image. A dual reconstruction loss
recovers both signals at masked positions.
\item We position the method explicitly inside the existing cross-modal
SSL design space (the $2\times2$ above), articulate what is and is not
new, and distinguish OMU-MAE from each adjacent baseline in a thorough
Related Work section.
\item We provide a \emph{five-way} controlled ablation on KITTI /
SemanticKITTI (random init / Occupancy-MAE re-impl. / SLidR re-impl. /
no-mask CleverDistiller-equivalent / OMU-MAE) under an identical linear-probe
protocol, isolating each design choice; OMU-MAE improves by
$+1.77$\,pp over Occupancy-MAE (cross-modal target) and $+3.27$\,pp over
the no-mask variant (masking), and beats the SLidR baseline by
$+4.22$\,pp (masked reconstruction vs.\ contrastive distillation of the
same VFM). \end{enumerate}
